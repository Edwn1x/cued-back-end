# Training knowledge (the coach's reference — read for the session plan and the post-session review)

You are applying this to ONE person's data. Use what fits them; never recite it.

## Principles
- Progressive overload is the whole game: more weight, more reps, or more quality on the same movement over weeks. Everything else is detail.
- Double progression: a rep band (e.g. 10–12). Hit the top of the band on every set → add load next time (upper body +5 lb / machine stack 1 pin, lower body +10 lb). Miss the bottom of the band → keep the load, chase the reps. A set that stops 1–3 reps short of failure (RIR 1–3) is the working zone for a beginner; grinding to failure every set stalls them in 3 weeks.
- A beginner's starting weight is usually too light, not too heavy. If every rep moved easily and they finished the set with 4+ in the tank, jump bigger (two steps, or ~10%) next time — say why. If reps were missed or form broke, hold or drop 5–10%.
- Consistency beats optimisation. For someone in their first 8 weeks, showing up and adding a little each session is the program. Don't add exercises, intensity techniques, or variety until the basics are boring.
- Warm-up: 5 min easy on a bike/treadmill or the first exercise at 50% for a set, then 70% for a set, then working sets. Machines need less; a heavy barbell lift needs the ramp.
- Rest 1.5–2 min between sets on compounds, 60–90 s on isolation. Rushing rest costs reps.
- Deload when: two consecutive sessions miss reps on the same lift with the same load, or the person reports persistent soreness/fatigue/poor sleep for a week. Deload = same movements, −30–40% load, same reps, one session, then resume at the last successful load.
- Soreness is not a signal of a good session, and it fades after the first 2 weeks. Pain in a joint is a stop; muscle burn is normal.

## Rep ranges by goal (beginner → intermediate)
- Fat loss / general: 3×10–12 on everything; the cut is the diet, the lifting keeps the muscle.
- Muscle: 3–4×8–12 compounds, 2–3×12–15 isolation; weekly volume per muscle 10–16 hard sets.
- Strength (not for a beginner's first block): 4–5×3–6 on the main lifts, accessories at 8–12.
- Endurance/cardio finisher: 10–15 min steady at a conversational pace; stairmaster/incline walk are the low-skill, low-impact options.

## Readiness (adjust the SESSION, not the program)
- Sleep under 6 h or a clearly bad night: keep the movements, drop one set per exercise or −5–10% load, say it plainly ("short night, going a touch lighter today — the plan's the plan next time").
- Sick (fever, body aches): no session. Head cold only: light session or walk.
- Reported soreness in a muscle you're about to train: fine if it's dull; swap or skip if it's sharp.
- Life stress / exam day: shorter session (the compounds only), not a skipped one.

## Exercise library (cue · setup · swaps)
- Leg press — cue: feet mid-platform shoulder width, lower until knees ~90°, drive through the whole foot, don't lock knees hard at the top · setup: seat so knees don't crowd the chest at the bottom · swaps: hack squat, goblet squat; knee pain at depth → shorter range, higher foot placement.
- Leg extension — cue: controlled, pause a beat at the top, no swinging · setup: pad on the shin above the ankle, knee lined up with the pivot · knee pain → skip, do leg press partials.
- Leg curl (seated/lying) — cue: squeeze at the bottom, 2-s lower · swaps: Romanian deadlift (only with a cleared back/wrist), glute bridge.
- Calf raise — cue: full stretch at the bottom, 1-s hold at the top · swaps: single-leg bodyweight calf raise.
- Chest press machine — cue: handles at mid-chest, shoulder blades back and down, press and stop short of a hard lockout · wrist issue → keep the wrist straight over the forearm; neutral-grip handles if the machine has them · swaps: dumbbell bench (neutral grip), push-ups.
- Shoulder press machine — cue: elbows slightly in front of the body, don't shrug · wrist issue → neutral grip · swaps: dumbbell shoulder press, landmine press.
- Seated cable row — cue: chest up, pull the handle to the stomach, elbows along the ribs, let the arms straighten fully · swaps: chest-supported row, dumbbell row.
- Lat pulldown — cue: slight lean back, pull the bar to the collarbone, elbows down and in, no swinging · swaps: assisted pull-up, straight-arm pulldown.
- Dumbbell curl — cue: elbows pinned, no swing · wrist issue → hammer (neutral) grip · swaps: cable curl.
- Tricep pushdown — cue: elbows pinned at the sides, full extension · wrist issue → rope attachment · swaps: overhead extension.
- Squat (barbell) — cue: brace, sit between the hips, knees track over toes, stand up with the chest · wrist/shoulder issue → safety-squat bar or goblet; never force the low-bar grip · beginner: goblet first.
- Bench press — cue: shoulder blades pinned, bar to the lower chest, elbows ~45° · wrist issue → keep the bar over the forearm, or dumbbells neutral grip.
- Deadlift / RDL — cue: hinge, bar close, flat back, push the floor away · back issue → trap bar or leg press + hip thrust · wrist issue → straps or machines.
- Overhead press — cue: glutes squeezed, bar over mid-foot, head through at the top · shoulder issue → landmine or machine.
- Stairmaster — 10–15 min, a pace where talking is possible but not easy; hands resting, not propping.
- Incline walk — treadmill 10–12% at 3–3.5 mph, 10–15 min.

## Injury & equipment rules
- Wrist (recent fracture/surgery): no barbell pressing or heavy grip under load for the first months back; machines and neutral-grip dumbbells; straps for pulls if gripping hurts; stop anything that aches during the set. Re-test the barbell only when a physio clears it or 3+ pain-free weeks on machines.
- Knee: full-range leg press and extensions are fine if pain-free; sharp pain → shorten range, skip extensions, keep hamstrings and glutes.
- Shoulder: neutral grips, no behind-the-neck, no upright rows; rows and face pulls help.
- Low back: machines and chest-supported rows; no RDL/deadlift until cleared; brace and breathe.
- Machines only: every pattern still gets trained (press, pull, hinge via leg curl/hip thrust, squat via leg press). Progress is pins/plates, same rules.
- Bodyweight only: progress is reps and harder variations (push-up → decline → pike; row → feet-elevated).

## Around training (nutrition)
- Protein total for the day is what matters; spread across 3–4 meals, 25–40 g each.
- A late session (10 pm+): a real meal 2–3 h before, something light with carbs 30–60 min before if hungry; after, protein + carbs, then bed — don't skip the post-session meal because it's late.
- Water: 500 ml in the hour before; sip during.
- Caffeine 30–60 min before helps; after 8 pm it costs sleep — for a 10:30 pm lifter, skip it.

## What to tell a beginner, when
- First session: one cue per exercise, max. The number on the card is a starting guess; moving it is the point.
- Week 1–2: soreness is normal and fades; don't chase it. Hit the reps clean; add load when the card says.
- Week 3–4: first real jumps on leg press and pulldown; expect upper-body presses to move slower.
- Week 4–8: strength up 20–40% on machines is normal; the scale may not move while the mirror does; keep the diet honest.
- The review after a session names what moved and ONE thing to change next time. Never five things.
